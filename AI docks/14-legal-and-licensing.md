# 14 — Legal and licensing

## This project's licence: GPL-3.0

Not a free choice. **eSpeak NG is GPLv3**, and we link it into the plugin, so
the combined work must be GPLv3. `LICENSE` holds the full text.

Practical consequences:

- Anyone we distribute a `.3gx` to is entitled to the corresponding source.
  Keep the public repository in step with any binary release.
- Every source file should carry a short GPLv3 header.
- We cannot add a dependency whose licence is incompatible with GPLv3.
  Permissive licences (MIT, BSD, zlib, Apache-2.0) are fine to pull in;
  proprietary or GPL-incompatible ones are not.

## What must never be committed

This repository must contain **no copyrighted Nintendo, Game Freak, or
The Pokémon Company content**. Specifically prohibited:

- ROMs, cartridge dumps, CIA/CXI/3DS files.
- `code.bin`, `.code`, or any ExeFS content.
- RomFS dumps, GARC archives, or files extracted from them.
- Ripped game text, dialogue, or Pokédex entries.
- Ripped audio, music, sprites, models, or textures.
- Save files (also a privacy concern — they contain personal data).
- Keys, seeds, xorpads, or anything else that facilitates decrypting a title.

What **is** fine to commit:

- Memory offsets and addresses. Facts about where a value sits are not
  creative expression.
- Structure definitions and format documentation.
- Our own factual name tables (species, move, item, and ability names are short
  factual labels, generated by our own tooling — see the caveat below).
- Research notes, disassembly observations, and analysis.
- All of our own code.

### The caveat on name tables

Species and move names are short factual identifiers, and reproducing a list of
them is the sort of thing tools like PKHeX have done publicly for years. It is
still Nintendo's trademarked vocabulary. Position taken by this project:

- Generate name tables with our own tooling, from publicly documented indices.
- Keep them minimal — names only, no flavour text, no Pokédex entries, no
  dialogue.
- If a rights-holder objects, remove them and switch to runtime RomFS reading
  (option 1 in `04-gen6-reverse-engineering.md`), which ships no data at all.

Pokédex entry text, NPC dialogue, and item descriptions are **creative content**
and are never shipped — they are read from the player's own game at runtime.

## Players supply their own game

The mod is a runtime overlay. It patches nothing on disk and modifies no game
files. It requires the player to already own and have installed the game.

This project:

- Does not distribute games or game content.
- Does not circumvent copy protection.
- Does not help anyone obtain a game they do not own.
- Does not link to ROM sites, and will not accept contributions that do.

Documentation in `docs/` should assume a legally owned game and say so.

## Custom firmware

Installing this mod requires Luma3DS on the player's own console. Modifying a
device you own is generally lawful in most jurisdictions, but:

- Never present CFW installation as risk-free. Link players to the established
  community guides rather than writing our own.
- Never bundle CFW, boot files, or firmware in a release.
- Note in `docs/` that the player is modifying their own hardware at their own
  risk, and that online play with CFW can carry a ban risk.

## Accessibility and the ethics of it

Worth stating plainly: the purpose here is to let blind people play games they
have bought, on hardware they own. That is the entire motivation, and it is why
rule 2 in `CLAUDE.md` matters — the moment this becomes a cheat tool, it stops
being an accessibility project and loses that footing.

Keep the boundary sharp:

- Speak what a sighted player can see. Nothing more.
- No RNG manipulation, no stat editing, no item generation, no hidden-data
  reveals.
- Anyone wanting those has other tools; point them there and keep this one
  clean.
